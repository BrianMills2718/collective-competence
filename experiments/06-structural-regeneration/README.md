# Experiment 06 — structural regeneration needs boundary information

This is the first specimen here with **actual component loss and regrowth** rather than state repair on a fixed set of sites. It asks a deliberately narrow question: what extra information lets local proliferation distinguish a wound edge, where growth should resume, from a normal tissue edge, where growth should stop?

## System

A 64-site one-dimensional arena contains a 24-cell tissue occupying sites **20–43**. Cells can only proliferate into an immediately adjacent vacant site; there is no teleportation, centralized fill operation, movement, or cell death.

Two fixed organizer fields are sourced at the arena boundaries and decay exponentially. The position-aware controller does not use either absolute concentration. It uses the local ratio

`p = R / (L + R)`

and permits a birth only where `0.2 <= p <= 0.8`. At equal source strength that defines the 24-site tissue. The organizer fields are external positional information for this experiment; they are not regenerated by the tissue.

Two controls matter:

- **single:** an absolute threshold on the left gradient, calibrated to produce exactly the same 24-site morphology at source amplitude 1;
- **ungated:** any vacancy adjacent to tissue can be filled.

## The local-information ambiguity

With occupancy alone, a normal right tissue edge and the surviving edge after a right-side amputation have the same radius-1 occupancy signature:

`(occupied_left, occupied_self, occupied_right) = (1, 1, 0)`

So a translation-invariant deterministic radius-1 growth rule that sees only occupancy cannot choose “stop” at the normal boundary and “grow” at the wound boundary. Some additional information must break that symmetry. The positional fields are one deliberately simple way to do it.

This is a logical information-boundary argument, not an empirical claim that morphogens are the only solution.

## Formation and common-mode robustness

Starting from one central cell, the ratio controller forms the exact 24-site tissue at common source amplitudes **0.75, 1.0, and 1.25**. Common multiplication of both signals cancels from the ratio.

The single-gradient control is exact only at its calibration amplitude 1. At amplitude 0.75 it forms sites **15–37**; at 1.25 it forms **25–47**. The ungated controller fills all 64 sites.

That contrast is mostly algebraic rather than surprising: relative encoding removes common-mode source amplitude, whereas an absolute threshold does not.

## Regeneration after amputation

Left-end, right-end, and middle amputations remove **4, 8, or 12 cells** from the established target. Across all 27 combinations of damage geometry, damage width, and common source amplitude:

- **ratio:** 27/27 recover the exact sites 20–43, with exactly one birth per removed cell;
- **single:** 9/27 recover exactly — only the nine amplitude-1 calibration cases;
- **ungated:** 0/27 recover exactly and every case eventually fills all 64 sites.

Examples show the two ways the absolute cue can fail. At common amplitude 0.75 after an 8-cell right amputation, the single-gradient tissue ends at sites **15–37**: it grows past the normal left boundary while leaving the distal right target unrepaired. At amplitude 1.25, the same damage ends at sites **20–47**, restoring the missing right side but overgrowing beyond it.

So actual cell replacement is easy in this toy once a candidate site can be classified as belonging to the morphology. The scientifically useful question is the information required for that classification and stopping decision.

## Boundary of the ratio solution

The ratio code is invariant to **common-mode** source changes, not arbitrary organizer changes. Holding the left source at 1 while changing the right source shifts the recovered morphology: right amplitude 0.75 gives sites 20–46; 1.5 gives 16–40; 2.0 gives 13–37. Removing either source entirely prevents any births in the declared right-amputation test.

Thus the result is not “two gradients make regeneration robust.” It is narrower: this relative positional code rejects one nuisance variable (shared source amplitude) but depends on the relation between its organizers.

## Scope and interpretation

This is a white-box constructive result. The target band and positional decoding rule are authored, so the experiment does **not** show autonomous goal discovery, biological regeneration, or a tissue inventing its own morphology. It also does not establish that two morphogens are minimal; other boundary cues, mechanics, memories, or collective signals could resolve the same local ambiguity.

Its value is to expose a clean information problem created by structural loss: local occupancy does not tell a normal boundary from a wound boundary. External positional information solves that problem here, and relative rather than absolute encoding gives a specific robustness benefit.

The next harder question is therefore endogenous: can the tissue generate or maintain the information that tells it how much to regrow, instead of reading a fixed external coordinate map?
