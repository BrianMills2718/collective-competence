# Research questions

[Wiki home](index.md) · [Findings](findings.md) · [Current work](current.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

## Central question

**How do simple component capabilities and interactions produce robust, goal-relative competencies of a whole, and what can we infer about those competencies and candidate goals from the system's behavior?**

The programme approaches that question from two directions. **Collective Competence** constructs systems and varies their mechanisms. **Goal and Competence Discovery** observes and intervenes on systems and asks what goal criteria and competence claims the behavior supports, contradicts, or leaves underdetermined. The same Dynamical Laboratory supports both.

Neither arm requires a unique scalar notion of "more competent," and Goal Discovery does not require there to be one uniquely identifiable true goal. A result may support several candidate criteria or remain underdetermined. The scientific burden is to state what the evidence actually discriminates.

## Constructive questions

1. **What minimal mechanisms produce a system-level competency?** Start with small systems where the mechanism can be inspected and changed directly.
2. **Which component capabilities and couplings are load-bearing?** Remove, weaken, replace, or vary one thing at a time where possible.
3. **What makes successful behavior persist under changed conditions?** Distinguish reaching a criterion from maintaining or restoring it after disturbance.
4. **Where are the boundaries of the competency?** Noise, damage, heterogeneity, changed initial conditions, resource limits, and altered topology are useful because failures constrain the explanation.
5. **Which effects survive across different specimens?** A mechanism observed in sorting is a finding about sorting until a meaningfully different system shows the same relationship.

## Discovery questions

1. **Which candidate goal criteria are supported by behavior without supplying the semantic answer to the analyst?** A candidate should earn support through behavior beyond the observations used to propose it.
2. **What distinguishes active goal-directed performance from passive convergence or an attractor?** Interventions are especially useful when ordinary trajectories look alike.
3. **Which aspects of competence can actually be demonstrated?** Attainment, maintenance, reliability, efficiency, robustness, recovery, adaptation, flexibility, and transfer are separate measurements; experiments need only claim what they test.
4. **When is the evidence underdetermined?** Several goal descriptions may remain behaviorally equivalent under the available observation and intervention contract. That is a legitimate result, not a failure to force a label.
5. **What additional observation or intervention would discriminate rival explanations?** This is useful when rival candidate goals or mechanisms currently make the same predictions.

## Current concrete programme

The founding sorting system is the first serious specimen. It already shows that local interactions can produce a global competency without the target being represented inside the agents, that reaching a target differs from continuing to steer toward it, and that perturbations expose boundaries invisible in a successful trajectory.

The immediate scientific priority is to add **qualitatively different specimens at roughly the same level of tractability**, rather than making the research apparatus substantially more elaborate. The first target is a minimal system that gives passive convergence, negative-feedback regulation, compensation, and later adaptation a concrete home on the shared substrate.

The existing D1-D6 register in [goals.md](goals.md) remains the detailed historical/current source for the discovery phase. This page is the compact working map; if it conflicts with a native protocol or committed result about what was actually tested, the native evidence wins.

## Questions deliberately not required

- A universal scalar ordering of systems by "competence."
- A proof that a system has one and only one true goal.
- A universal substrate for every possible system.
- A demonstration that every interesting collective effect is emergent in a strong philosophical sense.
- More formalism or governance unless a concrete research question needs it.
