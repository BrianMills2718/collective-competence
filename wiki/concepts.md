# Concepts

[Wiki home](index.md) · [Questions](questions.md) · [Full ontology](ontology.md)

This is the working vocabulary for ordinary research navigation. The full [ontology](ontology.md) remains the detailed authority when a precise definition, formalization, alias, or historical distinction matters.

## Alignment with Levin/TAME vocabulary

The project is deliberately **Levin-compatible by default**. When discussing Levin/TAME work, use that literature's vocabulary rather than inventing parallel labels. Use the project's finer terms only when an experiment needs additional measurement or evidentiary precision.

In particular:

- **Problem space** is a first-class term here. A representation defines the coordinates in which a system can be described as navigating toward, maintaining, or recovering a criterion.
- Levin uses **competency** broadly across scales. This project subdivides that vocabulary for measurement: **capability** names a bounded operation, while **competence** names graded goal-relative performance across a declared challenge/resource family. This is an operational refinement, not a disagreement with Levin's broader usage.
- **Intelligence** is used in the William James/Levin sense of reaching the same goal by different means in a declared problem space. In this ontology that corresponds most directly to the **flexibility** dimension of a broader competence profile; do not use intelligence as a synonym for the entire profile.
- **Goal** and **goal-directedness** do not imply consciousness or an internal symbolic target. For Goal and Competence Discovery, however, the project uses the more explicit term **candidate goal criterion** and requires enough evidence to distinguish the proposed interpretation from passive convergence, invariance, or artifacts before promoting it. That is an evidentiary rule for this programme, not a rejection of Levin's graded treatment of goal-directedness.
- **Memory** is not defined as one persistent hidden variable. Persistent internal state is one possible memory mechanism; memory may be distributed, structural, physiological, morphological, or otherwise realized. Do not call NCA hidden channels "memory" without an intervention that supports that interpretation.
- **Collective Competence** is the name of this project's constructive arm/property. When describing Levin's work, prefer his own terms such as **collective intelligence** and **multiscale competency** where those are the terms used by the source.

The detailed crosswalk and any genuine divergences are maintained in [the ontology](ontology.md#levins-definitions-alongside-ours). If a future result appears to require a vocabulary different from Levin's, state the experimental reason rather than silently creating a synonym.

## Focal system and boundary

A **focal system** is whatever collection of state and components the experiment treats as the system of interest. Its **boundary** states what is inside and outside. The same physical component can be inside one study's boundary and part of the environment in another.

Boundary and scale are choices that must be declared when they affect attribution.

## Mechanism

A **mechanism** is the causal organization that produces behavior: update rules, feedback, memory, morphology, coupling, communication, learning, scheduling, and similar structure.

Knowing a mechanism can explain a result, but a mechanism description is not itself evidence that the system is competent toward a goal.

## Capability

A **capability** is a bounded thing a system or component can do under stated conditions: sense, transform, remember, communicate, move, regulate, repair, and so on.

The constructive arm asks how capabilities and their organization combine to produce system-level behavior.

## Observation and representation

An **observation** is information the analyst is allowed to use. A **representation** transforms those observations into coordinates in which patterns, distances, relations, or candidate goals can be described.

A representation can create apparent regularity, so a pattern in a chosen coordinate system is not automatically evidence of a goal.

## Goal criterion

A **goal criterion** states what counts as success for the focal system in the chosen representation. It can be a point, region, relation, invariant, trajectory property, viability condition, morphology, distribution, or other condition on states or histories.

Calling something a goal does **not** imply consciousness, intention, planning, or an internal symbolic objective.

In a constructive experiment the criterion may be authored by the experimenter. In Goal Discovery, the interesting case is a criterion proposed from permitted observations and then supported, contradicted, or left underdetermined by further evidence.

There need not be one uniquely identifiable true goal. Multiple candidate criteria can remain compatible with the available evidence.

## Challenge family

A **challenge family** is the set of initial conditions, perturbations, damage, noise, environmental changes, resource limits, or other tests over which performance is examined.

A claim about competence is only as broad as the challenge family actually tested.

## Competence

**Competence** is goal-relative performance across a declared challenge and resource family.

Useful dimensions can include:

- attainment or maintenance;
- reliability;
- efficiency or cost;
- flexibility of means;
- robustness to perturbation;
- recovery after disruption;
- adaptation through change;
- transfer to new conditions.

These dimensions do not need to collapse to a universal scalar. The programme generally cares more about the measured profile and its limits than about declaring one system "more competent" overall.

## Robustness, recovery, and adaptation

These should not be conflated.

**Robustness** means performance remains acceptable across perturbation or uncertainty.

**Recovery** means performance returns after disruption.

**Adaptation** requires some change in behavior, policy, organization, or mechanism that restores or improves performance. A fixed system that repeatedly relaxes back to an attractor can be robust and recover without adapting.

## Collective competence

A **collective competence** claim attributes goal-relative performance to a chosen whole whose components and interactions jointly produce the behavior. It does not require that every component share the same goal representation, or that the global criterion be explicitly represented inside the system.

The constructive question is mechanistic: what component capabilities and couplings are required for the whole's competency, and how does changing them alter the result?

## Goal and Competence Discovery

**Goal and Competence Discovery** asks, under a declared observation/intervention contract, which goal criteria and competence claims are supported by behavior.

A good result may be:

- a supported candidate goal;
- several surviving candidate goals;
- a bounded competence profile toward a supplied or inferred criterion;
- a rival passive explanation;
- or an explicit `underdetermined` / `abstain` result.

Discovery is not a requirement to force semantic labels onto every dynamical system.

## Substrate and laboratory

The **substrate** is the executable realization that produces the dynamics. The current shared substrate is a generalized one-dimensional discrete interacting lattice that can express the founding sorting system and constrained cellular automata.

The **Dynamical Laboratory** is the broader apparatus for constructing/importing systems, observing them under controlled access, perturbing them, branching counterfactual runs, and measuring outcomes.

## A useful discipline

Keep these questions separate:

1. **What happened?** — observed trajectory/result.
2. **What can the system do?** — capability or competence claim.
3. **What counts as success?** — goal criterion.
4. **Why did it happen?** — mechanism.
5. **How strongly is that interpretation supported?** — evidence and alternatives.

Most conceptual mistakes in this programme have come from collapsing two of those questions into one.
